Which certificates do I need?

A plain-English guide to the common safety certificates and checks for rented homes and business premises in England.

Rented homes

What landlords in England typically need for a residential let.

Certificate / checkHow oftenNotes
Gas Safety Certificate (CP12)Every 12 monthsRequired where there are gas appliances. Carried out by a Gas Safe registered engineer; tenants must get a copy.
EICR
Electrical Installation Condition Report		At least every 5 yearsRequired for private rented homes. Any remedial work found must be completed.
EPC
Energy Performance Certificate		Valid for 10 yearsNeeded to let or sell. Rented homes must usually meet a minimum rating.
Smoke & CO alarmsChecked at the start of each tenancyA smoke alarm on every storey, and a carbon monoxide alarm in rooms with a fixed combustion appliance.
Legionella risk assessmentReviewed regularlyLandlords must assess and control the risk from Legionella in the water system.
Fire risk assessmentReviewed regularlyRequired for HMOs and the common parts of flats.

Business & commercial premises

Common requirements for offices, shops, schools and other non-domestic buildings.

Certificate / checkHow oftenNotes
Fire risk assessmentReviewed regularly, and after changesA legal requirement for non-domestic premises; must be written down if you have 5 or more employees.
Fire alarm servicingUsually every 6 monthsPeriodic inspection and servicing, in line with BS 5839.
Emergency lightingMonthly test, plus an annual full testInspection and certification in line with BS 5266.
EICRTypically every 5 yearsShorter intervals for some premises types.
PAT testingBased on riskNo fixed legal interval, but electrical equipment must be kept safe; testing is the usual way to show this.
Gas safety checkUsually every 12 monthsFor commercial gas appliances and boilers.
Fire extinguisher servicingEvery 12 monthsAnnual service in line with BS 5306.

This guide is general information for England and isn't legal advice. Requirements differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and may be stricter under an HMO licence, a lease or your insurer's conditions. Ask us and we'll confirm what applies to your building.

Common questions

What happens if my property fails an inspection?

You'll get a clear explanation of what failed and why, and a quote for the remedial work. Once it's fixed, the certificate can be issued.

Do I need to be there for the visit?

Not necessarily. We can arrange access directly with your tenants, agent or site contact.

Can you manage several properties?

Yes. A compliance contract covers a whole portfolio, with one schedule showing every certificate and its due date.

How will I receive my certificates?

By email as a PDF, and with a contract they're also kept on file for you.

Not sure what applies to you?

Tell us about the building and we'll confirm what's needed.
